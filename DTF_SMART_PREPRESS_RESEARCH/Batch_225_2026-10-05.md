## Engineering synthesis
- Preserve a soft alpha matte as an intermediate representation; do not immediately force every background-removal result to binary alpha. Hair, smoke, glass and other mixed pixels need per-pixel opacity during extraction.
- DTF output needs a separate print-safety decision. Partial alpha can drive a visible white-underbase fringe, so the preflight engine should quantify partial-alpha pixels and offer an explicit hardening/threshold stage rather than silently modifying the master.
- Threshold is not universally safe: hard alpha can staircase low-resolution edges. Keep threshold strength configurable and evaluate edge detail before/after.
- Add alpha-edge trim as a morphological operation distinct from background segmentation. Expose erosion/choke in physical or pixel units and preserve enclosed holes (letters O/D/R) using connectivity/hole logic.
- Underbase should be derived from the final print mask and choked slightly inside the CMYK boundary. Treat underbase generation as a RIP/output concern, not as destructive editing of the source artwork.
- Halftone must enforce a physical minimum feature/dot size. A current DTF-oriented implementation warns below about 0.15 mm and exposes LPI, angle, gamma, transition zone and minimum dot size; our preflight should model dot size in mm at target print dimensions rather than rely on screen pixels.
- Garment-aware separation can be luminance-based for black garments or color-distance-based for colored garments; halftone can be confined to a transition band instead of globally damaging solid artwork.
- Background removal, alpha cleanup, upscaling, resize and halftone order matters. Store non-destructive stages and rerun from source when target size changes.
- Browser-local processing is useful for upload privacy and avoids server-side retention, but large-image memory/performance limits still need explicit safeguards.
