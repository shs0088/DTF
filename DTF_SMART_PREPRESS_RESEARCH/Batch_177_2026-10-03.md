## Engineering synthesis additions

- Morphological cleanup must be scale-aware. A structuring element that bridges a 3-pixel gap at one scale may fail or destroy legitimate structure at another. Store physical-scale-equivalent kernel geometry, not only pixel radius.
- Add a **Skeleton/Stroke Preservation QA** pass before and after aggressive alpha morphology. Compare skeleton topology, endpoint count, branch count, connected components and local distance-to-boundary thickness. This detects when cleanup silently breaks thin lettering or line art.
- Hit-or-Miss provides a deterministic way to detect known binary neighbourhood patterns and underlies thinning/pruning. It can support targeted artifact detection rather than global erosion/opening.
- Skeleton/medial-axis distance values provide a useful estimator for local half-stroke thickness. A printability guard can reject or warn when morphology/choke reduces local stroke width below the calibrated output-condition survivability threshold.
- Thinning and skeletonization are analysis tools here, not output transformations: do not replace printable artwork with a skeleton. Use them to compare topology and feature survival.
- Preserve endpoint semantics during pruning. Short branches can be noise, but blindly pruning them can remove intentional serifs, accents, whiskers, hair strands or small text features.
- Provenance additions: morphology_kernel_shape, morphology_kernel_physical_size, skeleton_algorithm, skeleton_connectivity, endpoint_count_before_after, branch_count_before_after, local_medial_radius_min, pruning_rule, pruning_iterations.
