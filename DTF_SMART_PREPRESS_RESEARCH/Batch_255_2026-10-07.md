## Engineering synthesis additions
1. **Separate neutral alpha analysis from production white transfer.** Keep a neutral alpha-derived white eligibility map for diagnosing the artwork, then apply the target RIP/print-mode transfer function separately. This prevents production settings from hiding defects in source alpha.
2. **Make underbase QA a first-class view.** The operator should be able to inspect Original, Alpha/Mask, Neutral White, RIP White, and final garment composite independently. A visually clean garment preview is not sufficient proof of a clean white plate.
3. **Use physical-unit edge control internally.** Pixel choke is resolution-dependent. Store choke/spread in mm (plus calibration provenance), then convert to pixels only after final physical size/output resolution are fixed.
4. **Protect thin features by topology, not by a global erosion exception.** Before choke, compute local thickness/distance transform and connected components. Limit local erosion so mandatory text strokes, serifs and halftone islands retain a calibrated white spine.
5. **Lock screening to physical output size.** A halftone bitmap must not be resized after generation without invalidating dot-size/LPI assumptions. If later resizing is requested, regenerate screening from the continuous-tone master.
6. **Distinguish alpha fringe from RGB contamination.** Correct choke cannot repair antialiased pixels carrying old-background RGB. Run boundary-band contamination analysis/decontamination before RIP calibration.
7. **Calibration must vary one factor at a time.** Generate controlled wedges/sheets where artwork and all other settings are fixed while choke, white density, or screening changes independently; persist the winning setting with printer/RIP/print-mode/media provenance.
8. **Vector-derived masks can improve geometric stability but are not a universal raster-art solution.** Use vector-derived underbase only when tracing is faithful; photos, hair, smoke and fractional-alpha artwork must preserve continuous coverage rather than being forced through vector/binary geometry.
