## Engineering synthesis
- Halftone preparation should be calibrated to the actual output process. Brandum exposes AM/FM/diffusion screening, tonal preprocessing, minimum/maximum printable dot, dot-gain compensation, underbase choke, thin-line protection and multi-garment-color preview. Its calibration-wedge approach reinforces storing measured min-dot/max-dot/dot-gain per output profile rather than relying on universal defaults.
- Thin-line protection should be topology/feature-width aware. A fixed erosion can remove white support from narrow strokes; adaptive choke should preserve a centerline/spine or locally reduce choke when the feature cannot survive the requested physical contraction.
- Distressed artwork needs morphology/topology QA, not merely alpha cleanup. Tiny detached ink islands should be removed when below calibrated survival size, tiny holes may need filling, and intended knockout regions should be truly transparent. Distress dimensions should be evaluated in physical units at final print size.
- RIP interoperability requires explicit channel semantics. Named W1 spot channels, embedded ICC profiles, transparency preservation, compression choice and hard/soft white-edge policy should be provenance fields rather than inferred from file extension.
- Upscaling QA must distinguish source information from generated/interpolated detail. Desired physical print width and effective source DPI should be evaluated before enhancement; post-upscale pixel dimensions alone must never convert a low-information source into an automatic pass.
- Multi-background preview remains mandatory: white, black and representative garment colors expose RGB fringe and white-underbase halo failures that checkerboard-only review can miss.
