## Engineering synthesis
- White underbase optimization is multi-objective: opacity, edge coverage, fine-detail survival, transfer hand, ink usage and durability. White density and choke/spread must remain independent parameters and be calibrated per output condition.
- Screening must record its family. AM varies dot size on a structured screen; FM varies dot frequency; hybrid mixes behavior. The correct choice is output-condition dependent and should be validated by controlled physical tests rather than assumed.
- A calibration wedge that measures the lightest and darkest reproducible dots provides a practical press-specific min/max dot survivability envelope. Preflight should compare generated halftone features against that calibrated envelope.
- Halftone preparation is geometry-dependent: LPI/frequency, output DPI and final physical width jointly determine raster dot geometry. Any resize after screening invalidates the prior printability assessment.
- Browser-local processing is viable for privacy-sensitive prep, but client memory constraints become part of the engineering envelope for large final-size/high-DPI rasterization.
- Korean printer documentation reinforces that nominal printer DPI and pass count are device/output-mode properties, not evidence that a low-resolution source contains equivalent image detail.
