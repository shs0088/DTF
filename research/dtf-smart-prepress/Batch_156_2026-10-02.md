## Engineering synthesis

### 1. Resampling provenance must distinguish pixel geometry from print geometry
Image scaling changes pixel count; print size can change through resolution metadata without changing raster samples. Smart Prepress must therefore keep source pixel dimensions, final physical dimensions, effective PPI and resampling history as separate fields.

### 2. Upscaling is not evidence recovery
GIMP explicitly states that interpolation creates missing samples but does not add new detail. Any upscale candidate remains derived evidence and must not upgrade source-quality confidence merely because its pixel dimensions are larger.

### 3. Repeated transforms are a quality risk
Repeated layer scaling can progressively blur raster content. The pipeline should prefer one transform from the highest-quality source directly to final geometry, and record transform count/history so iterative resize chains can be penalized or rejected.

### 4. Partial masks are semantically valuable
Converting a grayscale layer mask to a selection preserves intermediate gray values as feathered/partial selection membership. This reinforces preserving fractional alpha/soft masks through cleanup and mockup preparation rather than binarizing them early.

### 5. Scale operations need explicit interpolation and crop policy
Interactive scaling exposes interpolation, crop and aspect-ratio behavior as independent choices. Smart Prepress should persist these choices in provenance and rerun topology/text/edge checks after the transform.
