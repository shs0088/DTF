1587. https://www.pictoonline.com/aide/profils-icc — French professional-print ICC workflow guidance, opened/read directly. It distinguishes embedded working-space identity from output-profile conversion, supports perceptual versus relative-colorimetric plus black-point-compensation choices, warns that untagged RGB/CMYK/gray files force assumed profiles, and ties soft proofing to calibrated viewing conditions. Engineering transfer: Smart Prepress must separate `ASSIGN_PROFILE` from `CONVERT_PROFILE`, preserve embedded-profile provenance, make rendering intent/BPC explicit at conversion, and flag untagged files rather than silently treating an assumed profile as source truth.

It reports that computational cost can rise materially with large white-ink choke values and that a RIP optimization specifically accelerated white-ink/choke processing. Engineering transfer: choke is not merely a visual scalar; it changes RIP workload. Preflight/job estimation should therefore record choke geometry and consider computational complexity for very large raster masters, while never weakening physical-edge QA just to gain speed.

1589. https://nestsheet.com/white-underbase — White-underbase implementation documentation, opened/read directly. Engineering transfer: underbase generation needs an inspectable intermediate artifact, not a hidden RIP side effect. `WhiteGeometry`, `AlphaThreshold`, `WhiteAmount`, `SoftHandMode`, and `WhiteScreening` should remain separate provenance fields, and the exact generated white channel should be QA-visible before export.

## Engineering synthesis
1. Add a strict color-state machine: `UNTAGGED -> ASSUMED_FOR_DIAGNOSIS` must not silently become authoritative source color. `ASSIGN_PROFILE` changes interpretation without changing pixel values; `CONVERT_PROFILE` changes values to preserve appearance. Store source profile, destination profile, intent and BPC separately.
2. Treat soft proof as a view artifact, not master-pixel evidence. Record proof profile and calibrated viewing assumptions independently from the print master and never bake proof colors into the master unless explicitly requested.
3. Promote the generated white channel to a first-class `WhiteArtifact` that can be previewed, hashed and QA-tested before RIP/export. White geometry, threshold, density/amount, soft-hand behavior and screening must not collapse into one slider.
4. Add RIP-cost awareness to white processing. Large choke/spread and high-resolution white masks can materially affect processing time; job estimates should include image area/resolution and white-mask transform complexity without changing print-safe settings automatically.
5. Preserve physical-unit authority. Choke/spread must be represented in physical units or be traceably converted from pixels at a known output resolution; any later resize invalidates the derived pixel radius and requires regeneration plus minimum-feature QA.
