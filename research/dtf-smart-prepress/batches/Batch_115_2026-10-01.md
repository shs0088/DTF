1568. https://printunite.com/background-remover — PrintUnite background-removal pipeline. Read directly. Separates semantic segmentation, alpha matting/edge refinement, and RGBA export; explicitly targets fine hair/fur/semi-transparent fabrics and notes that different model classes have different edge capability. Engineering transfer: model/backend identity belongs in matting provenance; route hard silhouettes separately from micro-detail/fractional-alpha assets; evaluate edge quality independently of coarse segmentation accuracy; keep mockup-safe RGBA output as a separate acceptance target from print-master acceptance.

1569. https://docs.gimp.org/zh_CN/gimp-filter-mean-curvature-blur.html — Chinese GIMP Mean Curvature Blur documentation. Read directly. Mean-curvature regularization smooths geometry at a rate related to local curvature while preserving edges and can be used for denoising; iteration count increases smoothing. Engineering transfer: add curvature-preserving smoothing as a deterministic denoise candidate for line/shape artwork, but gate it with component/hole/minimum-stroke and alpha-boundary displacement metrics because preserving a perceptual edge does not guarantee preservation of print-critical topology.

1570. https://docs.gimp.org/3.0/fr/gimp-filter-gaussian-blur-selective.html — French GIMP Selective Gaussian Blur documentation. Read directly. Blur is applied only where neighboring-value differences remain below a configurable maximum delta, so high-contrast boundaries are protected while similar regions are smoothed. Engineering transfer: represent denoisers by spatial radius plus similarity/range threshold rather than a single strength slider; use this family for low-frequency grain/noise in flat regions while protecting text/contours, and measure edge displacement after processing.

1571. https://support.flux3dp.com/hc/pt-br/articles/9402670389647-3-6-A-ret%C3%ADcula-FM-e-a-ret%C3%ADcula-AM — Portuguese FLUX AM/FM screening guidance. Read directly. AM varies dot size on a fixed grid; FM keeps dot size more constant while varying dot density/spacing. The guide observes AM can provide smoother coverage in broad neutral regions while FM can retain more fine texture/hair detail and recommends a small production test. Engineering transfer: screening selection should be content-aware and production-profile calibrated; large flat regions and fine-detail regions may have different optimal screening families, so a hybrid/region-aware policy is preferable to a universal screen.

1572. https://uhl-media.de/cms/fm-und-am-raster-fuer-high-end-druckprodukte — German AM/FM screening explainer. Read directly. AM uses fixed positions and varying dot size; FM varies dot frequency with equal-sized dots and is selected according to substrate. It notes reduced moiré tendency for FM but possible disadvantages in uniform raster areas. Engineering transfer: add `ScreeningRisk = MOIRE | FLATNESS_NOISE | DETAIL_LOSS | MIN_DOT_FAILURE`; screening QA must inspect both texture/detail retention and large-area uniformity rather than treating moiré avoidance as the only objective.

## Engineering synthesis
1. Split background removal into coarse semantic segmentation, fractional-alpha refinement, foreground-RGB decontamination and export QA. Record model/backend/version because edge behavior differs materially across inference systems.
2. Add deterministic denoise routing by artifact and structure: mean-curvature smoothing for geometry-aware regularization; selective/range-aware Gaussian smoothing for low-contrast noise while protecting strong boundaries. Both require topology and edge-displacement regression checks.
3. Replace a generic `DenoiseStrength` with explicit provenance: `family`, `spatial_radius`, `range/similarity_threshold`, `iterations`, `channel_scope`, and final geometry. Any resize invalidates pixel-scaled denoise parameters.
4. Expand screening provenance to `AM | FM | HYBRID | ERROR_DIFFUSION` plus dot geometry/frequency, physical output size, substrate/garment assumptions and calibration profile.
5. Add screening QA dimensions for moire, flat-field noise/mottle, fine-detail loss, minimum-dot survival and directional/periodic artifacts. A screen that suppresses moire can still fail on broad uniform coverage or transferability.
6. Prefer content-aware or region-aware screening selection: broad neutral/flat areas and hair/texture/fine-line regions may benefit from different screening behavior. Hybrid screening should therefore be a first-class profile option rather than an afterthought.
7. Keep mockup-safe output and print-master output as separate acceptance targets. A visually clean RGBA cutout may still contain alpha/underbase behavior unsuitable for DTF, while a hardened print edge may be visually inferior for product mockups.
