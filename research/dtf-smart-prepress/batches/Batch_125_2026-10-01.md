## Engineering synthesis

### 1. White-underbase choke must be stored in physical geometry, not only pixels
NestSheet's choke glossary explicitly distinguishes choke (contract white underbase) from spread (grow it) and notes that pixel values depend on working resolution; its 2 px example at 300 dpi is about 0.17 mm. Smart Prepress should therefore canonicalize choke/spread to physical distance at final output geometry while preserving the original RIP/pixel parameter for provenance. Any resize after white-mask generation invalidates that calibration and requires regeneration.

### 2. Adaptive/local threshold maps are a first-class segmentation primitive
The Russian GIMP threshold-map article reconstructs a Niblack-like local threshold map from local mean/deviation information and shows that normalizing the source by that map gives more controllable threshold behavior than a single global cutoff. For DTF artwork this supports an `ADAPTIVE_THRESHOLD` route for uneven backgrounds, scanned art, signatures and distressed graphics, with window/radius, local-statistic family, coefficient and normalization strength recorded independently.

### 3. Morphology provenance needs border policy, origin, connectivity, mask and convergence semantics
SciPy's ndimage tutorial and binary-erosion reference make several parameters explicit that matter to reproducibility: structuring-element connectivity, origin placement, border value, optional operation mask, iteration count, and the special `iterations < 1` behavior that repeats until convergence. Smart Prepress should not reduce morphology to `radius + operation`; all of these parameters can alter thin strokes, holes and edge position and therefore must be part of the operation hash/provenance.

### 4. Distance transforms are useful for resolution-independent choke/spread and feature safety
The SciPy tutorial describes Euclidean, city-block and chessboard distance transforms. A distance-field representation provides a cleaner architecture for white-underbase geometry than repeated pixel erosion/dilation: generate a signed/inside-outside distance field at final geometry, offset by physical distance, then rasterize the requested mask. This also enables minimum-feature guards by measuring local inscribed distances before accepting a choke.

### 5. Deblurring must distinguish restoration from ordinary sharpening
The Russian restoration article explains why classical Wiener restoration can leave artifacts and motivates Total Variation priors that preserve sharp edges while suppressing deconvolution artifacts. For Smart Prepress, `DEBLUR_RESTORATION` must remain separate from `SHARPEN`: restoration requires a blur/PSF model plus regularization and can hallucinate/ring when the model is wrong; sharpening merely increases local contrast. After deconvolution, QA should re-check ringing/halo, glyph topology, edge displacement, minimum strokes and noise amplification before allowing a print master.
