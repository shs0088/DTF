## Engineering synthesis

### 1. Screening provenance must include scan path, pre-tone transform and palette construction
The open-source `slapash/dither` implementation exposes multiple error-diffusion kernels, ordered Bayer matrices, clustered-dot halftone, random thresholding, Riemersma path diffusion, fixed/custom palettes, median-cut auto-quantization and gamma before dithering. This reinforces that `ScreenArtifact` identity is not just the algorithm label: store diffusion kernel/path, ordered matrix, palette source/construction, quantization levels and any pre-screen gamma/tone transform. A tone change before quantization changes the resulting dot population and must invalidate prior screen QA.

### 2. Preview/downscale shortcuts cannot become print-master evidence
The same implementation notes that large pure-Python error diffusion can be expensive and suggests downscaling for speed, while ordered methods vectorize efficiently. Smart Prepress must distinguish `PREVIEW_SCREEN` from `PRODUCTION_SCREEN`: a downscaled preview can guide interaction, but final acceptance requires regeneration at final physical geometry and minimum-dot/minimum-gap/tone-conservation checks.

### 3. Classical upscaling plus sharpening remains non-reconstructive
Rasterbator explicitly states that browser smoothing/upscaling and sharpening can improve apparent edge cleanliness but cannot recreate missing detail. QREndless similarly separates bicubic, nearest-neighbor and Lanczos-3 resampling and applies optional unsharp masking afterward. Smart Prepress should therefore preserve `ResampleArtifact` and `SharpenArtifact` as separate stages and never promote classical enlargement to recovered-detail authority. Final QA must run after the sharpened export, not only on the preview.

### 4. Resource limits belong to image-processing provenance and security
QREndless caps output at 8192 px to avoid browser memory exhaustion and explains that nominal 4x scaling may be reduced to satisfy that cap. A pipeline must record requested scale separately from actual output geometry. Upload/prepress workers should enforce pre-decode byte limits, post-decode pixel/side limits and work-buffer/output budgets so a resource clamp cannot silently alter effective DPI or final physical size.

### 5. Alpha quality and foreground-color decontamination are independent
WuTools' French technical explanation distinguishes semantic segmentation from continuous alpha matting and explains halos as contaminated mixed edge pixels. Photomop makes the same compositing model explicit: good matting estimates both continuous alpha and foreground color; solving alpha alone can preserve the old background tint. `MatteArtifact` should therefore retain alpha plus foreground-RGB/decontamination evidence, with multi-background QA rather than checkerboard-only inspection.

### 6. Matting should be selectively routed by edge class and source quality
Photomop distinguishes hard-edged opaque products (often mask-sufficient) from hair, fur, smoke, glass, lace and motion blur (fractional-alpha cases), and warns that compressed/downscaled inputs damage exactly the boundary pixels matting depends on. Smart Prepress should classify boundary type before selecting binary segmentation vs matting, preserve the highest-quality source for inference, and reject low-resolution/compressed proxies as master evidence.
