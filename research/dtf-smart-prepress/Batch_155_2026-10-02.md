## Engineering synthesis

### 1. Halftone thresholds are OutputCondition evidence, not universal constants
The Spanish DTF source reports shop-specific observations for very light tones, stochastic screening, and creative halftone dot sizes. These values must be stored as calibration evidence tied to a printer/RIP/ink/film/garment condition, not promoted to universal pass/fail standards.

### 2. Screening remains RIP/output-stage authority
The same source explicitly makes RIP responsible for dot shape, angle and lineature. Therefore the general Ready-to-Print Master should preserve continuous-tone/alpha information unless a specific production contract intentionally requests a pre-screened artifact.

### 3. Gradient QA needs a low-population / isolated-dot diagnostic
A fade that approaches zero can turn into isolated color+white dots on dark garments. Preflight should measure low-alpha/low-tone populations and simulate the white-support consequence, rather than only checking whether a gradient is visually smooth on screen.

### 4. Choke must remain physical and output-specific
The Italian DTF source recommends RIP-side white choke and warns that excessive contraction destroys fine text/details. This reinforces final-geometry survival checks after white generation/choke and rejects hand-drawn generic white borders as a master-file substitute.

### 5. Denoising requires scale-aware, edge-aware controls
Vayce exposes noise scale relative to image size, edge protection, and separate color-noise behavior. Smart Prepress should represent denoise as an operation with noise class, spatial scale, edge-protection strength and final-detail survival metrics, not a single global “clean” slider.

### 6. Upload safety must include decoded geometry
Adobe’s Arabic official page exposes both file-size and maximum-dimension constraints. Prepress upload protection should enforce compressed bytes, decoded dimensions/megapixels, supported formats, processing time/memory and concurrency—not MB alone.

### 7. Simple-background removal deserves a deterministic route
The Arabic browser tool uses user-selected/background-color sensitivity and edge softness for homogeneous backgrounds. For simple studio/product backgrounds, deterministic color-distance + connected-border/flood-fill logic can be cheaper and more auditable than AI, while preserving protected interior whites.

### 8. Batch/model routing must not weaken per-image QA
The Arabic AI tool exposes quality/speed routing and batch processing. Model selection and scheduling are orchestration concerns; each resulting alpha/foreground artifact still needs independent provenance and final-resolution QA.
