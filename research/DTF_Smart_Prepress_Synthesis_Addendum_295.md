# DTF Smart Prepress — Engineering Synthesis Addendum 295

Date: 2026-10-09. Based on 20 newly opened/read technical sources listed in docs/DTF_SMART_PREPRESS_LEDGER_295_ADDENDUM.md. All proposals are pre-implementation hypotheses until regression and physical printing tests.

1. **Separate alpha coverage from edge RGB decontamination.** A transparent PNG may have a clean alpha but contaminated colors; incorrect straight/premultiplied interpretation can produce black/white fringes. Use a straight-alpha canonical master, store original RGB+alpha+ICC, and test compositions over black, white and colored backgrounds. German decontamination workflows modify RGB and should produce a reversible derived layer, not overwrite a master. Japanese Blender and PNG guides reinforce format mismatch risks.
2. **Preserve geometry/topology during morphology.** Japanese C++ OpenCV morphologyEx examples use repeated operations; Italian university slides define hit-or-miss, 4/8-connected isolated pixels, iterative region filling and grayscale morphology. Add pre/post component-count, hole-count, Euler characteristic, minimum stroke-width and boundary displacement tests. For Arabic designs, preserve dots/diacritics even when isolated.
3. **Distinguish mockup atlas bleed from printable RGB.** Japanese Spine texture-packing documentation discusses RGB bleed into alpha-zero pixels to prevent interpolation fringes. This may be appropriate for 3D/mockup texture sampling but must not modify the approved print master. Verify straight vs premultiplied alpha at the renderer boundary.
4. **Dithering must be mathematically tested, and not confused with RIP calibration.** A Rust/Python package documents exact error-diffusion families, ordered matrices, serpentine scanning, optional linear-light math and alpha preservation. Unit-test kernels, tone ramps, matrix uniqueness, edge behavior and alpha invariance. Neither preview quality nor coverage proves printed dot survival.
5. **Printer-specific color management and physical QA.** Russian prepress material correctly distinguishes device-specific CMYK from abstract CMYK; Brazilian textile screen-print research uses spectrophotometry and GLCM print-mottle metrics across washing cycles. Apply these as candidate DTF QA methods only after printer/film/adhesive/garment calibration. Do not assert universal 300 DPI, choke, or underbase values.
6. **Import safety and provenance.** The Korean OpenCV tutorial notes IMREAD_UNCHANGED and BGR order; Arabic interactive course repo offers useful testing fixtures. Validate decoded pixel dimensions, alpha channel and ICC separately, sandbox untrusted uploads, enforce resource/time limits and immutable originals. No external upload is required for preflight processing.
7. **Specific regression fixtures to add later:** (a) transparent PNG with straight/PMA mismatch, (b) bright text with interior holes and Arabic dots, (c) single-pixel strokes at multiple final sizes, (d) gradient glow with alpha 1..254, (e) texture atlas bleed vs untouched print master, (f) Bayer/FS/Atkinson ramps, (g) RGB/CMYK profile roundtrip, (h) dark garment white-underbase choke mockups checked against physical swatches.

**Important:** a Japanese halo article uses 'premultiplied alpha' as a broad explanation for retained matte color; these are distinct failure modes. Diagnose independently rather than adopting the terminology as a proof. White-only RIP behavior may differ from CMYK-underbase choke; a previously counted DTF knowledge-base article describes such an exception for its own workflow, not a universal rule.

Status: research only; no merge, deployment or protected storefront edits. Historical global semantic deduplication pending.
