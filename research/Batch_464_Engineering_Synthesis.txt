Progress: previous provisional 5265; nine new accepted pages; updated provisional upper bound 5274/10000 (52.74%); 4726 remaining by arithmetic. Global uniqueness is not certified.

Findings:
1. Keep Alpha geometry, foreground RGB decontamination, and RIP white-layer generation as separate outputs. Choke compensates for registration tolerance; it does not reliably fix semi-transparent fringe RGB. Do not binarize every Alpha channel: retain soft photographic edges unless a tested RIP workflow requires a binary/halftone derivative.
2. Express choke in millimetres as well as pixels: mm_per_px = 25.4 / DPI. Assign choke to either prepress or RIP, never both by accident. Test thin strokes, small text, circles and isolated dots.
3. Keep halftone LPI, angle, minimum dot, tone/gamma, knockout, speck cleanup and hole filling as separate controls. Vendor presets are not universal standards; validate after film, powder, curing and pressing.
4. Reduced model-input resolution can degrade the matte even if the output canvas is later enlarged. Track preview scale and use a validated high-resolution final path.
5. Diagnose halos by inspecting RGB and Alpha separately and compositing over white, black and saturated backgrounds. This separates capture-time spill from code-level fill-color artifacts.
6. Add script-aware fine-detail checks. Arabic diacritics, ligatures and small disconnected marks must not be deleted solely because of component area.
7. Check model license and benchmark domain before production use. The AEMatter repository says noncommercial use/distribution only.
